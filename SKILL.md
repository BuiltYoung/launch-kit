# Launch Kit

Made by Built Young (builtyoung.org). This credit is for people reading the skill. Never add it to a student's launch content.

Turns a working product into a ready-to-run launch: the right platform, every field written, the people lined up, and an hour-by-hour plan for the day. Built for Built Young GTM classes, where an instructor runs it with each team. Most founders here are under 18, so the eligibility step is not optional.

## Workflow

1. Intake
2. Pick the platform
3. Eligibility gate
4. Build the kit for that platform
5. Supporters and launch-day schedule
6. After the launch

### 1. Intake (ask only for what's missing)

- Product: name, what it does, who it's for, the link, and what state it's in (live, beta, working prototype, idea)
- Software or physical product
- Founder ages in brackets: under 16, 16 to 17, 18 or over. Which adult (parent, guardian or instructor) is involved
- Target launch date, and the founders' time zone
- Who already knows about it: waitlist size, testers, friends, clubs, family
- What exists: logo, screenshots, demo video, real quotes with permission, real numbers

### 2. Pick the platform

| Situation | Launch here |
|---|---|
| Software that people can use today through a link | Product Hunt, plus a school or community launch |
| A physical product that needs money to make the first batch, with a working prototype | Kickstarter, run by an adult creator |
| Audience is mostly local (classmates, a school, a neighbourhood), or founders are under 16 with no adult ready to hold an account | School and community launch |
| Not usable yet | Don't launch. Open a waitlist and come back when one stranger can use it without help |

A team can do more than one. A school launch a week before Product Hunt is good practice and finds the bugs.

### 3. Eligibility gate

State the result of this step at the top of the kit, before any copy. The rules below were checked against the official pages in November 2026. Platforms change them, so when web access is available, re-read the linked page before quoting a rule, and trust the page over this file.

**Product Hunt** (producthunt.com/legal, producthunt.com/launch)
- Launches come from personal accounts with a verified email. Company and brand accounts can't submit.
- The terms require the account holder to be of legal age to form a contract, or to have a parent's or guardian's permission with the parent agreeing to the terms. The privacy policy says the service is not intended for children below 16.
- So: 18 or over, launch from your own account. 16 to 17, your own account with a parent's or guardian's permission. Under 16, an adult launches from their own account as the hunter and the student is credited in the maker comment by first name, with the parent's consent.

**Kickstarter** (help.kickstarter.com, "Who can use Kickstarter?")
- The creator must be 18 or older. Under-18s can take part only under the supervision of the creator.
- The creator needs to live in an eligible country (Canada and the US are both on the list), with government photo ID from that country, a bank account in their own name there, and a payment card in their name.
- So: for a student project, a parent, guardian or the program is the creator. That adult is legally responsible for the money and for delivering every reward. Make sure they understand this before any copy is written.

If no eligible adult is available, switch to the school and community launch. Never suggest entering a false age or borrowing an account without the owner taking part.

### 4a. Product Hunt kit

Write every field to its limit and show the character count after each one.

| Field | Limit | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Name | 40 characters | The product name only. No tagline, no emoji |
| Tagline | 60 characters | What it does, in plain words. No "best", no "#1" |
| Description | 500 characters | The value first, then the features that deliver it, the problem, and who it's for |
| Launch tags | Up to 3 | Choose from Product Hunt's own list |
| Logo | Square, 240x240 recommended, JPG/PNG/GIF up to 2MB | Must read at small size |
| Gallery | At least 1 image, aim for 3 or more, 1270x760 recommended | Show the product in use, not the logo |
| Video or demo | Optional | A screen recording with narration is enough |
| Product link | Direct link, no link shorteners | Test it in a private window |
| Pricing | Free / Paid / Free options | Match the team's pricing model |
| Promo code | Offer up to 25 characters, code up to 15 | Only if the team will honour it |
| Maker comment | Up to 5,000 characters, pinned first | See below |

**Maker comment.** Aim for 120 to 200 words, written the way the founder talks: who we are, why we built it (the moment the problem became real), what it does, who it's for, what's different, one specific question we want feedback on, and any offer. A student founder's real story is the strongest thing on the page; an age or grade may be mentioned only if the founder and parent are comfortable with it.

**Rules that get launches removed.** Ask for feedback, never for upvotes: Product Hunt lists asking for upvotes as spam. Never pay for votes, trade votes, or use a service that promises them. Being featured on the homepage is an editorial decision and is not guaranteed, so never promise a team a ranking.

**Timing.** A launch can be scheduled up to 30 days ahead and goes live at 12:01 AM Pacific Time on the chosen day, running the full day. Convert that to the founders' time zone in the kit (for Toronto, 3:01 AM). Only schedule when the page is complete, because scheduled launches are reviewed.

### 4b. Kickstarter kit

Start with the delivery check, because a funded campaign is a promise to make and ship real things.

**Delivery check (do the arithmetic and show it):**
- Cost to make one unit, at the quantity the goal would fund
- Shipping and packaging per reward, by region
- One-off costs: tooling, samples, safety testing if the product needs it
- A buffer of 10 to 20 percent for what goes wrong
- Fees: Kickstarter takes 5 percent of funds raised plus payment processing (in the US, 3 percent plus $0.20 per pledge), charged only if the project is funded. Check kickstarter.com/help/fees for the creator's country.
- Funding goal = the total of the above for the smallest batch worth making. If the team can't fill in a number, write `[NEEDS: ...]` and stop there until they can. A goal built on guesses is how student campaigns end up owing people products they can't afford to make.

**How funding works.** It is all-or-nothing: if the goal isn't reached, backers are not charged and the team gets nothing. A campaign can run 1 to 60 days, and Kickstarter says around 30 days tends to work best.

**Then write:**
- Title and one-line subtitle: what it is and who it's for
- Story: the problem, how the idea started, the prototype today, what the money pays for, the timeline, the team
- Reward tiers as a table: price, what the backer gets, delivery month, cost to fulfil, margin. Three to five tiers, including one low tier for supporters who just want to help
- Video outline, 60 to 90 seconds: the problem, the prototype working in a real hand, what backing unlocks, the ask
- Risks and challenges, written honestly: what could delay delivery and what the team will do about it
- Pre-launch page text, so supporters can follow before the campaign opens

**Prototype rules.** Show the real prototype doing what the page says it does. Kickstarter does not allow renderings that could mislead backers about what exists today, or CGI used to demonstrate how the product works. If the team only has renders, they are not ready for Kickstarter yet.

### 4c. School and community launch kit

- A 30-second announcement script for assembly, homeroom or a club meeting
- A one-page poster with a QR code to the product or waitlist (the ig-poster-studio skill can make it)
- A five-minute live demo plan: one problem, one task shown end to end, one ask
- A short message a parent can send to family and community group chats
- A note to the teacher or club lead asking for permission and a time slot
- A way to collect feedback on the spot: three questions on a form

Check the school's rules on promoting products and collecting sign-ups before anything goes up.

### 5. Supporters and launch-day schedule

**First ten supporters.** Make a table: name, how they know the product, how to reach them, who on the team contacts them. These are people who have used it or asked about it, not a class list. Write two short messages in the founder's voice:
- A week before: the date, what's launching, and that their honest feedback on the day would help.
- On the day: "It's live. Here's the link. We'd love to hear what you think." No request to upvote.

**Launch-day schedule.** Write it in the founders' local time and around the school day, since students can't reply during class:
- Before school: check the page is live, send the supporter message, post the main announcement
- During school: the adult on the team watches comments and flags anything urgent. Students reply at lunch
- After school: reply to every comment, post one update with a real number, thank people by name where they've agreed to it
- Evening: write down what happened while it's fresh

For the posts themselves (warm-up, launch day, and the two weeks after), hand off to the build-in-public skill.

### 6. After the launch

Produce a short recap the team can reuse: what they launched and where, visits, sign-ups or pledges, the three most useful pieces of feedback, what broke, and what they'd change. Real numbers only. This recap is the traction slide for a pitch competition and the next build-in-public post. If the launch was quiet, say so in the recap; a quiet launch with honest lessons is still usable evidence.

## Output format

Deliver one kit in this order: **Eligibility result** · **Platform and date** · **Page copy** (each field with its character count) · **Assets to make** (a checklist with sizes) · **Supporters** (table and two messages) · **Launch-day schedule** · **Open items** (every `[NEEDS: ...]` in one list, with who should supply it).

Keep commentary short. The kit is the deliverable.

## Honesty and safety

- Never invent users, numbers, quotes, press mentions or awards. Use `[NEEDS: ...]` for anything the team hasn't supplied.
- Describe the product as it works today. A beta is a beta; a prototype is a prototype.
- For founders under 18: first names only unless a parent has agreed to more, no school name without the school's and parent's permission, no personal phone numbers or home addresses anywhere public, and an adult has access to every account used.
- Quotes and photos of other people need their permission, and a parent's if they are under 18.
- No fake urgency. "Only 20 early spots" is fine if it's true and will be honoured.

## Language

Reply in the user's language. Write page copy in the language of the platform's audience (English for Product Hunt and Kickstarter). Write the school and community materials in the language that community uses, and write each version natively, not as a translation.
